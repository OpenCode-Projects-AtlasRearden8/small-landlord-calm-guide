# Chapter 7 — Records to Keep

Records are the superpower of the tiny landlord. Companies have systems; you
have, so far, a shoebox and a text thread. This chapter replaces the shoebox
with one folder structure, one naming habit, and one retention table.

> Education only, not legal or tax advice. Retention rules vary by state and
> by tax authority, and change. This chapter uses conservative round numbers —
> keep more rather than less.

## The habit: one folder per tenancy

```
records/
  2026-maple-st/
    01-application-and-screening/
    02-lease-and-amendments/
    03-inspections/            (move-in, mid, move-out photos + signed sheets)
    04-rent-and-payments/      (receipts, bank proof)
    05-repairs/                (requests, replies, invoices)
    06-notices/                (rent hike, entry, deposits, terminations)
    07-deposit/                (itemization, refund proof)
    08-legal-and-tax/          (1099s, receipts, tax returns)
```

That's it. Every e-mail, PDF, photo, and reply goes inside folder
`20XX-address/` — never loose. Inside the folders, name files so they sort by
date: `2026-03-01_movein_bedroom1_NEwall.jpg`. Sortable beats searchable when
you're stressed.

## The long list (keep for years)

| Record | Where it lives | Keep for (conservative) |
|---|---|---|
| Signed lease + every amendment | Folder 02 | 7+ years after tenancy ends |
| Move-in inspection + photos | Folder 03 | 7+ years (travel with the lease) |
| Move-out inspection + photos | Folder 03 | 7+ years (deposit disputes, tax) |
| Deposit itemization + refund proof | Folder 07 | 7+ years |
| Rent payment proof | Folder 04 | 7 years |
| Repair requests, replies, invoices | Folder 05 | 7 years |
| Screening applications (including rejects) | Folder 01 | 2–3 years (fair housing recordkeeping) |
| Notices sent (rent hike, entry, entry denial, termination) | Folder 06 | 7 years |
| Property tax bills, insurance, mortgage statements | Folder 08 | 7 years |
| Repair receipts, capital improvements | Folder 08 | 7 years after you sell |
| Tax returns for the rental | Folder 08 | 7 years after filing |
| Incident reports, police/insurance claims | Folder 08 | 7 years |

Why conservative? Real-world rules differ (small claims limitations often run
2–6 years; tax record rules around 3–7; discrimination claims have their own
windows). More disk space is cheap; a gap is not.

## What to log every month (fifteen minutes)

Open your records folder and log, in one spreadsheet or notebook page:

- rent received (date, amount, method);
- any expenses paid for the property (date, vendor, amount, what);
- repairs both applied for and completed;
- anything written to the tenant (notice, request, complaint);
- a photo or two if anything changed.

If a year went by and you did this fifteen-minute habit twelve times, you'll
be ready for a deposit dispute, a tax filing, and an insurance claim, all with
the same folder. If you skipped the habit all year, you'll be reconstructing
history from a text thread with a witness who stopped picking up.

## Digital or paper?

Digital, mostly — but **paper is required** for the things that feel real:
the signed lease original, signed inspection sheets, and anything you'd turn
over physically (keys, remotes — yes, log remotes; they get lost).

Photograph paper documents, keep the PDFs of everything, and back up the
folder in two places (a cloud drive plus an external drive every quarter).
Local-first, no personal-data paranoia: your tenant's names and IDs should
never leave your own storage; never store a tenant's SSN in an email and
never keep a photo of a **full** ID in anything unencrypted.

## One more kind of record: the written memo

When a disagreement starts, write a quiet note to yourself the same day:
date, very short facts, no adjectives. Not to send — to keep. *"3/4: tenant
texted asking to delay March rent to the 15th. I agreed in text, no fee."*
That entry is the one that saves you at small claims, not the argument,
because the file — your files — corroborate everything.

## Privacy footnote (because they're real people)

Tenants are real people, and their data deserves care:

- keep their files under your control (not a shared drive they can reach),
- strip any photo containing license plates, minors' faces, or mail,
- never keep full bank account numbers or SSNs in plain documents,
- when the tenancy ends for long enough, folders name a person you once knew.
  Keep the records. Treat them like records, not gossip.
