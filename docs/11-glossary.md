# Chapter 11 — Glossary

Plain-English definitions for every term this manual uses. Words your lease
or your state's law uses are italicized in the source chapters; this list is
the decoder.

**Abandonment** — When a tenant leaves without paying notice, returning keys,
or otherwise signaling they've moved out. Handles the question "can I clear
out their stuff?" — the answer depends on your state's procedure (often a
written notice and a waiting period), so check.

**ADU** — Accessory Dwelling Unit. A small second home on a single-family
property: garage conversion, basement unit, backyard cottage, in-law suite.

**Amendment** — A signed written change to the lease. Never just texts and
handshakes.

**Assistance animal** — A service animal (ADA language) or emotional-support
animal (fair-housing language). Legally *not* a pet; see Chapter 4.

**Assignment** — When a tenant hands their lease entirely to someone else.
Different from subletting, and usually both need your written consent.

**Capital improvement** — Spending that makes a property *better* (new roof,
new kitchen), deducted slowly through depreciation, not all in the year paid.

**Capitalization (basis)** — The tax amount your property "costs," including
purchase price plus improvements, minus depreciation taken so far. Drives
gain when you sell.

**Casualty loss** — A sudden, unusual loss from an event (fire, storm).
Insurance recovery and tax treatment have their own rules; ask a pro.

**Common area** — Shared space (hallway, yard, laundry room) in a multi-unit
or rooming situation. Responsibility for cleaning/maintaining is by lease.

**Companion animal** — Another name for an emotional-support animal
(Chapter 4's framework applies).

**Curable default** — A lease violation that can be *fixed* (late rent paid,
behavior corrected), which usually triggers a written notice and a deadline
before further action.

**Default** — A lease violation (usually non-payment) that, after proper
notice, can lead to termination and eviction through the courts.

**Depreciation** — The paper deduction (roughly building basis ÷ ~28 years
for residential) that reduces taxable rental income each year. Chapter 8.

**Depreciation recapture** — When you sell, some of what you deducted comes
back as taxable gain, often at a special rate up to about 25%. Talk to a CPA
before selling.

**Deposit (security deposit)** — Money you hold from the tenant, against
unpaid rent and damage beyond normal wear, returned with an itemization
within your state's deadline (Chapter 2).

**Disclosure** — Required written information (lead paint, flood risk, mold,
rental registration and so on) that federal, state, or local law makes you
give the tenant. Look up your state's list.

**Eviction** — A *court-ordered* removal of a tenant. Not a lock change, not
a signal from your gut. Chapter 1's "remedies" clause points to this process.

**Fair housing** — Federal (and state and local) law forbidding discrimination
in housing: HUD's seven protected classes, plus more locally. Chapter 4.

**Fixed-term lease** — A lease with a stated length (12 months, say), after
which it either renews or ends by its own terms.

**Grace period** — Time past the due date before rent is late. Not
automatic-Federal — check your lease and your state.

**Habability** / **habitability** — The minimum condition a rental must meet
to be legally livable (heat, water, safe structure, working locks and alarms).
Can't be waived in the lease. Chapter 6.

**Holdover** — A tenant staying after the lease ends. Usually converts to
month-to-month at the old terms, or a contractual holdover rate if stated.

**Indemnify** — Lease language where one party agrees to cover the other's
costs in a certain situation. Read these sentences slowly.

**Itemization** — The written list of deductions you send with (or instead
of) a deposit refund. Required in most places, in writing.

**Key money** — Some landlords charge extra for a key or parking spot; legal
status varies wildly. Avoid anything that sounds like a hidden deposit.

**Late fee** — Money for paying after the grace/deadline. Must be reasonable
and, in many states, tied to your actual cost. Enforce it or don't — but
don't threaten daily.

**Lease** — The written contract for the rental. Chapter 1, clause by clause.

**Lien** — A legal claim against the property (a mortgage is one; so are some
unpaid-contractor judgments). Affects who gets paid first if things go badly.

**Lessor / lessee** — Landlord / tenant. Old words, same people.

**License vs. lease** — A *license* is permission to use (like a storage
unit); a lease gives possession. Some city rental-registration regimes
license the *unit* regardless.

**Month-to-month** — A lease renewing monthly until either party ends it with
proper notice (typically 30 days; more for larger rent hikes in some laws).

**Normal wear and tear** — The kind of aging that happens when a place is
merely lived in. Not chargeable to the deposit (a common misconception —
Chapter 2's "would I have paid anyway" test).

**Notice to quit / notice to vacate** — Written notice that the tenancy ends.
Your state's law sets who must give it, how, and how far ahead.

**Quiet enjoyment** — The tenant's right to use the home without the landlord
hovering. Entry rules (Chapter 1, clause 7) exist to protect it.

**Reasonable accommodation** — A change or exception you make for a person
with a disability, so they can use and enjoy the home equally (a parking
spot, an assistance animal, allowing a grab bar). Chapter 4.

**Retaliation** — Punishing a tenant for exercising legal rights (complaining,
repairing on their own and deducting where allowed, joining a tenant group).
Many states presume retaliation within a short window. Chapter 3.

**Severability** — If one clause is illegal, the rest of the lease survives.

**Security deposit** — See deposit.

**Severance of tenancy** — When one roommate moves out and the remainder
figure out who is on the lease and who is not. Usually handled with a signed
amendment, not a hallway conversation.

**Self-help eviction** — Illegal almost everywhere: changing locks, removing
doors, shutting utilities off, tossing property, without a court order.

**Side contract** — An unwritten or texted agreement layered on the lease.
Enforceable (sometimes), unwieldy (always). Prefer a written amendment.

**Sole and exclusive possession** — What a lease promises (usually) — your
tenant's right to be there without you walking in. An owner's genuine
occasional need doesn't trump it; proper notice does the work.

**Subletting** — When a tenant rents out (usually) the whole unit to someone
else while keeping some connection to the lease. Needs your consent, usually
in the lease (Chapter 1, clause 10).

**Subject-to (sale)** — Selling a property with the tenant and lease and
deposit-liability still attached. The buyer inherits it all, including the
duties (Chapter 9).

**Tenancy at will** — Informal tenancy (often month-to-month, no fixed
lease). Ends with notice, not with feelings.

**Time is of the essence** — Lease phrase meaning dates actually matter.
Treat deadlines as deadlines (Chapter 10).

**Waiver** — Choosing not to enforce a right *this time*. Enforceable enough
to be a real signal; make it clear in writing so there's no precedent
argument either way.

**W-9** — The form a contractor fills out *before* you pay them $600+ this
year, so you can 1099-NEC them correctly (Chapter 8).

**1099-NEC** — The form you send that contractor (and the IRS) reporting
what you paid them — "NEC" for nonemployee compensation (Chapter 8).
