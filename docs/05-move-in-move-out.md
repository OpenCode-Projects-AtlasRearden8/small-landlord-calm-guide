# Chapter 5 — Move-In and Move-Out Checklists

This is the chapter that ends arguments. The trick of the whole tenancy is
embarrassingly simple: **photograph everything, in timestamped form, at both
ends**, and have both of you sign the same sheet.

> Education only, not legal advice. Some states have specific rules about
> move-in inspection rights (a tenant's chance to note damage before the
> deposit has to be handled), and some give tenants a right to a pre-move-out
> walkthrough with an opportunity to fix issues. Look up your state's rules
> and use the sheet below with those rules in mind.

## Why this works

Deposit disputes are usually not fights about *facts* — they're fights about
*memory*. Photos are memory that never gets emotional. Timestamped, geotagged
photos at move-in are the fence around the property line of your whole
relationship with this tenant.

## The move-in walkthrough (do this before keys change hands)

**Bring:** your phone (camera), the printed checklist, a pen, a roll of
blue painter's tape (to mark spots worth photographing), and 30 minutes of
unhurried time.

Walk every room together, outside in, and do these things:

1. Read every meter (or smart-meter app) and write the numbers down.
2. Photograph **every wall, floor, ceiling, window, and door** — wide shot
   plus close-up of anything that's already imperfect.
3. Test every light fixture, switch, outlet and faucet; note the state of the
   tub and shower grout.
4. Open every window and every appliance door. Photograph inside the fridge
   and oven.
5. Check smoke/CO alarms exist and appear to work (and log it).
6. Find the water shutoff and breaker panel, and show them (this is the
   tenant-education part of the visit — bring the laminated one-pager).
7. Note existing damage *in words* as well as photos ("nail hole in NE wall
   of bedroom, 3 feet above floor").

Both of you initial each page. Both of you get a copy — paper and a photo of
the paper. That's the standard.

Then: **every photo goes in the tenant's folder** in your records system
(Chapter 7), named like `movein_bedroom1_NEwall_2026-03-01.jpg`. One folder per
tenant, forever.

## During the tenancy

The single best habit: **take a photo of anything that changes as soon as it
changes**, and email it to the tenant with a caption. Leak under the sink?
Photo, date, plumber visit. Tenant's dog scratched a door? Photo, date, note.
Nobody is arguing in eighteen months.

## The pre-move-out walkthrough (offer it generously)

Many states give tenants the right to a pre-move-out inspection and a chance
to fix problems before deductions happen (Chapter 2). Even where it's not
required, offering it is good for everyone:

1. Offer it about two weeks before move-out, in writing.
2. Walk through together with the move-in photos on your phone, side by side
   with today's reality.
3. List anything a deposit deduction *might* cover, so they can fix it
   themselves before the final inspection.
4. Both of you sign the two-visit sheet.

Nobody has to feel surprised. That's the entire point.

## The move-out walkthrough (keys back, cash to follow)

1. Do it the day keys come back, or within a day or two — while facts are
   fresh and access is easy.
2. Bring the move-in checklist and photos again — compare, don't reconstruct.
3. Photograph the same shots with the same framing, so side-by-side is
   instant.
4. Get **all the keys** (front, back, garage opener, mailbox, the spare they
   forgot they were keeping) and write down exactly what was returned.
5. Get the tenant's **forwarding address** in writing. On the same day, if
   you can. This is required for deposit-return mailings (Chapter 2), and it's
   the one thing people forget at 5pm on a Saturday.
6. Read meters again (or app screenshots) for prorated final bills.
7. Do a fresh smoke/CO test at turnover and log it.

## Move-out checklist (the printable version)

- [ ] Pre-moveout walkthrough offered in writing (date sent: ____ )
- [ ] Move-in photos pulled up side-by-side during inspection
- [ ] All keys, openers, remotes, mailbox keys received and counted
- [ ] Forwarding address captured in writing
- [ ] Meters read (or app screenshots captured)
- [ ] Every room photographed, same framing as move-in
- [ ] Smoke/CO alarms tested and logged
- [ ] Tenant signature on move-out sheet (or note on why not — e.g., no
      response to two appointment offers)
- [ ] Deposit itemization started within your state's deadline
      (Chapter 2's habit applies to every step here)

## Room in your own home, special case

If the person was a housemate in your home, the walkthrough is still worth
doing — but gentler. Photograph shared rooms only where their storage or care
matters, and hand them the same two-page sheet so nothing feels like a trap.
Housemates who leave on good terms are also the people who say good things
about you when the next applicant asks around.
