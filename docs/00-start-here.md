# Chapter 0 — Start Here

Welcome. This manual is for people who rent out **one room, one house, or one
ADU** — not for people running a leasing office. If you've been putting off
landlording because it looks like a wall of legal jargon, you're in the right
place. We'll go through it like a friend would: plain words, one idea at a
time, and zero shame about what you don't know yet.

> **The one rule above all rules:** This guide is **education only**. It is
> never legal advice, and never financial or tax advice. Housing laws change
> from state to state, city to city, and sometimes block to block. Everything
> here uses round numbers and examples so you understand the *shape* of the
> answer — then you check your local rules or talk to a licensed pro before
> you sign, collect, or raise anything.

## The calm mindset

You don't need to memorize landlord law. You need to know four things:

1. **What type of question am I facing?** Paper (leases, notices), money
   (deposits, rent, taxes), people (screening, conflicts), or the building
   (repairs, inspections). Each has its own chapter.
2. **What's the deadline?** Most landlord trouble isn't caused by doing the
   wrong thing — it's caused by doing the right thing *late*. Our companion
   project, the lease-key-dates-tracker-webapp, exists purely for this.
3. **What's written down?** A text to your tenant that says "sure, take an
   extra weekend to pay" is a side contract. Write things down; sign things
   down; date things down.
4. **Would this survive being read out loud in small claims court?** Not
   because you're headed there — because imagining it makes your decisions
   cleaner and kinder.

## How to use this guide

- Read Chapter 1 (the lease) once. You'll never read a lease the same way.
- Treat Chapters 5 and 7 (checklists and records) as templates — copy them
  into your notes and use them today.
- Skim the rest when you hit that situation, coffee in hand.

## Free and frugal, always

Nothing in this manual requires a subscription. Where we mention forms, we
point toward **free public sources** — court self-help pages, state attorney
general tenant guides, and HUD's publications — and we assume you'd rather
print a PDF than buy software. (Chapters will say "find your state's version"
for anything where the details genuinely differ.)

## A story to keep in your pocket

A first-time landlord once lost a $800 deposit dispute — not because she was
wrong, but because she'd never written a move-in inspection and the ex-tenant's
memory of a "perfectly clean apartment" sounded just as convincing as hers.
Every chapter in this manual is designed so that story never happens to you.
Checklists beat memory. Dates beat vibes.

## Your first-week checklist

- [ ] Read Chapter 1 once, coffee in hand.
- [ ] Bookmark your state's free tenant/landlord guide (AG or court self-help site).
- [ ] Make the records folder (Chapter 7) for your property today.
- [ ] Put the key dates from Chapter 10 on a calendar (or the tracker app).
